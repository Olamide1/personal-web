Chat as an interface to LLMsNEW

July 31, 2026 • 1 min read

There is something about the current interface for LLMs (or AI), as we have chosen to call them, that does not sit right with me. My feel is that we have taken one use case's optimal UX and pushed/carried it across tools and un-applicable use cases that it now feels un-optimal and for the most part incomplete/almost lazy on the product developer's end.

My feeling and instinct is that the use of chat as a source has limited our ideas for what tools and usefulness should be and that is making it harder for its adoption. This does not include certain knowledge work (writing ,analytics - in some cases and work that requires producing written reports). For me, visual tools attempting to use chat as an interface (as a top-of-mind example) are severely missing the mark on opening up creativity for its users.

Here is my recommendation for how well we can create other sources of adoption and usage (some companies have done it to weird/mixed reactions because of the moral disagreements about LLMs and how much power they consume), but I still think they are more thoughtful:

1. In-context recommendations that are tied to end-users in the moment needs. Google's AI overview is doing a good job at that (even though there are some misinformation that permeates through it- That is an LLM-issue vs an execution issue. Canva, as a design product, has done a very good job of its AI incorporation in logo creation etc, but they are still in the chat an interface phase that honestly annoys me.

2. voice as the primary and only source of interaction. In my mind, working on products that require physical presence and activity, where your hands are busy, require audio and voice response as the primary source of engagement. Still chat, but different UX that fits the use case.

3. Good old checkboxes and buttons. Sometimes you know what the input is, you are trying to just make sure its unique to each individual case. I have seen and executed this while working on accounting/consulting services products and thankfully we did not fall into the trap of chat as the ultimate "AI" interface.

TLDR, think beyond the message box. We need more practical and creative ways to engage LLMs.

Cheers.
